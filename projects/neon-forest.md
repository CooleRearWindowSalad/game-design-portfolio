# Neon Forest

## The premise

Neon Forest is a study in contrast: luminous paths, uncertain landmarks, and systems that reward paying attention. The player follows a damaged signal through a living forest, learning its rules by observing what changes between visits.

## Design pillars

### Notice the change

Every return to the forest should reveal a small difference: a new path, a missing sound, or a light that now points somewhere else. Exploration is driven by attention rather than a checklist.

### Make uncertainty legible

The world can be mysterious without being unreadable. Color, sound, and repeated landmarks give the player enough information to form a theory before they act.

### Reward the long way around

The most direct route is not always the most useful one. Optional detours expose story fragments, environmental clues, and tools that make later discoveries easier to interpret.

## Open questions

- How much should the forest change between visits before the player loses their mental map?
- Which discoveries should be remembered by the character, and which should only exist in the player's notes?
- How can the signal feel urgent without turning exploration into a race?
